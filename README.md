# Move Media

Move Media is a service business for people and organizations built around movement, wellness, and community.

The initial focus is the dance sector: teachers, studios, event organizers, and the communities around them. Most of the same challenges also exist in yoga, fitness, and other movement-based activities, so the business may expand into those sectors as opportunities are validated.

Move Media combines web development, media expertise, workflow design, and automation to help movement professionals save time, become more visible, serve their communities better, and build more predictable businesses.

## Why Move Media exists

Movement professionals run many activities at once:

- Regular classes
- Online courses
- Workshops, competitions, performances, and other community events
- Product and merchandise sales
- Community communication and administration

Many are excellent teachers, performers, and organizers but have limited time, budget, or technical expertise for communication, media management, and business operations. Teachers in particular may hold a second job because their movement practice alone does not provide a sustainable income.

Their work often depends on irregular attendance, fragmented communication, manual administration, and personal financial risk. Visibility is essential, but producing content and maintaining digital tools can add even more work to already demanding schedules.

Move Media aims to close this gap with focused services and simple tools that produce practical business value.

## Mission

Help movement professionals spend less time struggling with technology and administration, and more time teaching, creating, and growing sustainable communities.

## Goals

Move Media will:

- Make the daily work of movement professionals easier
- Improve their visibility and communication
- Help them attract, retain, and better serve students
- Turn scattered information and media into structured, reusable assets
- Automate repetitive work where automation provides clear value
- Help small movement businesses make revenue more predictable
- Develop affordable solutions that can be delivered repeatedly and profitably

The immediate business goal is to validate, as quickly and economically as possible, whether Move Media can become a viable full-time company.

## People and organizations we serve

### Regular teachers

Professional teachers who work repeatedly with the same students, either independently or through one or more studios. They need dependable class attendance, simple student management, effective communication, and ways to identify opportunities for private coaching or additional classes.

### Event teachers

Professionals who teach different groups at workshops, festivals, conventions, or other events. They need strong personal visibility, clear schedules, reusable promotional content, and ways to maintain a connection with students after an event.

### Studio owners

People who bring teachers and students together in a physical or virtual studio. A studio may support one or many teachers and movement disciplines. Owners need class promotion, scheduling, student acquisition and retention, administration, and a coherent online presence.

### Event organizers

Professionals who coordinate teachers, venues, schedules, ticketing, communication, and promotion for one-time or recurring events. They need reliable workflows and consistent communication before, during, and after each event.

### Merchandisers

Businesses and independent creators selling clothing, footwear, accessories, or other products. Some products meet practical needs; others help people express their identity and connection to a community.

### Volunteers

Community members who support classes and events, often in exchange for free or discounted access. They need clear responsibilities, schedules, coordination, and fair recognition or rewards.

### Service providers

DJs, venue owners, photographers, videographers, sound and lighting technicians, and other specialists who make movement activities possible. They need visibility, professional coordination, and access to the right organizers and communities.

### Students and community members

Although professionals are the primary customers, students and community members are often the end users. Better discovery, communication, learning, media, and progress tracking should improve their experience as well.

## Potential services and products

The following areas define the opportunity space. They are not a commitment to build everything.

### Professional websites

Affordable, maintainable websites for teachers, studios, organizers, and service providers. A website should support a concrete outcome such as improving discovery, generating inquiries, filling classes, or selling tickets.

### Learning platforms

Simple online learning experiences for courses, class recaps, structured practice, and teacher feedback.

### Community websites

Local or topic-specific hubs that gather fragmented information in one place, including events, schedules, maps, teacher and studio directories, and community news.

### Content creation systems

Templates, methods, structured workflows, and tools that make it easier to plan, produce, reuse, and publish content consistently.

### Event communication

Event websites, registration or ticketing, schedules, promotional assets, and repeatable social media workflows.

### Community media tools

Tools that help students and teachers move class videos off their phones and into an organized media library. Possible capabilities include cloud synchronization, classification, annotation, sharing, teacher feedback, and social-media-ready exports.

### Progress tracking

Tools for students, teachers, or competitors to record goals, practice, feedback, achievements, and development over time.

### Student management

Lightweight systems for teachers to manage student lists, notes, feedback, follow-ups, and personal coaching opportunities while respecting student privacy.

## Founder advantage

Move Media is founded by a solo developer with experience in 3D animation production, media management, web and backend development, internal tools, and web-based mobile applications.

The central approach is to solve problems through structure:

1. Understand the real workflow.
2. Structure the information and media involved.
3. Remove unnecessary work.
4. Automate repeatable steps.
5. Turn successful solutions into reusable services or products.

This combination is especially relevant in a sector where media is abundant, information is fragmented, and much of the work is still manual.

## Initial strategy

Move Media will begin as a side project and grow through evidence rather than speculation.

### Phase 1: Learn and demonstrate

- Build one small, useful community project, such as a local dance information website
- Build one or two carefully selected studio or teacher websites
- Work closely with real users to understand their workflows and constraints
- Use completed projects as case studies and portfolio material
- Document the time, cost, outcome, and reusable parts of each project

Free or discounted work is acceptable at this stage only when it provides meaningful access, learning, evidence, visibility, or a reusable asset. Every pilot should have a defined scope and an explicit reason for being free.

### Phase 2: Find repeatable value

- Identify problems that recur across several professionals or communities
- Package successful work into clear, fixed-scope offers
- Reuse templates, components, processes, and automation
- Test willingness to pay before investing heavily in product development
- Prefer services that can later become productized or supported by software

### Phase 3: Build predictable revenue

- Develop recurring services where ongoing value is clear
- Add narrowly focused products only after demand has been demonstrated
- Build referral and partnership channels within movement communities
- Measure whether revenue, margins, and demand can support a transition to full-time work

## Operating principles

### Start with the problem

Do not build technology because it is interesting. Begin with a painful, frequent, and valuable problem experienced by a clearly identified customer.

### Stay focused

Movement is a broad market. Dance is the initial entry point, and only a small number of customer types and offers should be tested at once. New sectors or products should be added only when evidence justifies the added complexity.

### Prefer outcomes over deliverables

A website, app, or content template is not the final value. The value is a fuller class, a sold-out event, less administrative work, better student retention, or a more reliable income.

### Design for limited budgets

Solutions must be affordable for small organizations while remaining profitable to deliver. Reuse, standardization, narrow scope, and automation are essential—not optional extras.

### Keep services simple to buy

Each offer should make the customer, problem, scope, expected result, price, timeline, and ongoing responsibilities easy to understand.

### Validate before scaling

Use interviews, small pilots, prototypes, and paid commitments to test assumptions. Avoid large platforms until a smaller solution has demonstrated real demand.

### Build reusable assets

Every project should improve a shared library of knowledge, templates, software components, data models, workflows, or case studies. Custom work should contribute to future efficiency whenever possible.

### Use AI with judgment

AI should accelerate research, development, content operations, and automation. Human review remains necessary for accuracy, brand voice, privacy, cultural context, and decisions that affect a community.

### Respect trust and privacy

Student information, private feedback, photos, and videos can be sensitive. Collect only what is needed, make ownership and permissions clear, and treat security and consent as product requirements.

### Support the community

Move Media should strengthen the relationships that make movement communities valuable. Technology should support teachers, organizers, volunteers, and students rather than create unnecessary distance between them.

### Measure business value

Projects should be evaluated using evidence such as:

- Time saved
- Leads or registrations generated
- Class or event attendance
- Student retention and repeat purchases
- Revenue generated or made more predictable
- Delivery time and gross margin
- Customer satisfaction and referrals
- Amount of the solution that can be reused

## How opportunities are prioritized

An opportunity is more attractive when it:

- Solves an urgent and recurring problem
- Has a clearly identifiable buyer
- Produces measurable value
- Can be tested with a small investment
- Fits the founder's technical and media expertise
- Can reuse existing systems or become repeatable
- Can lead to recurring revenue or referrals
- Does not require a large audience to become viable

An opportunity is less attractive when it depends on speculative demand, extensive custom work, high support costs, unclear ownership of media or data, or users who benefit but have no realistic ability or willingness to pay.

## Current hypotheses

The project begins with several assumptions that must be tested:

- Small community websites can create relationships, visibility, and market insight
- A small number of studio or teacher websites can become strong portfolio case studies
- Professionals will pay for solutions that clearly save time or generate revenue
- Structured media and data workflows can become a distinctive advantage
- Repeated service work will reveal opportunities for templates, automation, and focused software products
- A combination of productized services and recurring tools can serve limited customer budgets while sustaining a profitable business

These are working hypotheses, not conclusions. Failed tests are useful when they produce a clear learning and prevent larger wasted investments.

## Near-term definition of success

The first stage succeeds when Move Media has:

- A small number of real users or clients in the dance community
- Completed work with measurable outcomes and credible case studies
- Evidence of which problems customers value enough to pay for
- At least one service that can be delivered repeatedly with a healthy margin
- A clearer path toward recurring revenue
- Enough demand and financial evidence to decide whether continued investment—or a future full-time transition—is justified

## Project status

Move Media is currently in its discovery and validation stage. The scope, positioning, and service catalog will evolve as work with real movement professionals produces better evidence.

## Repository structure

This repository is also the working knowledge base for the business:

```text
move-media/
├── actors/      Potential clients, partners, and other known actors
├── initiative/  Active directions, specifications, and delivery tracking
├── stories/     Factual observations and experiences from the field
└── thoughts/    Explorations, hypotheses, and possible solutions
```

Each area has its own guide and template. Use lowercase kebab-case names so folders and files remain easy to search and automate.

- A **story** records what happened or what was observed. It should distinguish evidence from interpretation.
- A **thought** explores what something could mean, how a problem might be solved, or what might happen next.
- An **initiative** is a direction chosen for active work. It defines a measurable outcome and follows the work through implementation and iteration.
- An **actor** records what is known about a real person or organization and the relationship with them.

A story can inspire one or more thoughts. A validated thought can become an initiative, and an initiative's releases can produce new stories and corrections. Any of these may identify actors to interview or serve. Link related records rather than duplicating their contents.
