# Step Up studio website

## Starting point

Move Media intends to build one or two early websites for real teachers or studios. Step Up is a possible subject for an initial studio website project.

The studio's current situation, goals, audience, digital presence, and interest in working with Move Media have not yet been documented in this repository. This thought therefore explores the opportunity without assuming that a redesign or new website is needed.

## Problem or opportunity

A studio website should help the studio and its students complete important actions. Possible problems to investigate at Step Up include:

- Prospective students cannot quickly identify the right class or level
- Schedules, prices, locations, and registration instructions are difficult to find or become outdated
- The studio receives repetitive questions through messages or calls
- Social media creates awareness but does not provide a stable path to registration
- The experience, atmosphere, and strengths of the studio are not clear online
- Events, schedule changes, and seasonal registration require repeated manual communication
- Website updates depend on a person, provider, or workflow that is too slow or burdensome
- Traffic and inquiries are not measured, making communication decisions difficult

These are investigation prompts, not claims about Step Up.

## Actors and users to understand

- The studio owner or business decision-maker
- Teachers and administrative staff
- Prospective students with no dance experience
- Experienced dancers comparing classes or levels
- Current students looking for practical information
- Parents or guardians, if the studio teaches children
- Event participants, if the studio organizes public events

The first discovery conversation should identify which of these audiences matters most to the studio's current business goals.

## Hypothesis

A focused studio website built around the path from discovery to registration can make Step Up easier to understand and join while reducing repetitive communication work.

As an early Move Media project, it may also:

- Demonstrate a results-oriented studio website service
- Reveal common studio content and data structures
- Produce reusable design, schedule, class, and teacher components
- Test a low-cost and repeatable delivery process
- Provide a measurable case study and referrals to other movement professionals

## Desired outcomes

Possible outcomes to validate with Step Up include:

- More qualified trial-class or registration inquiries
- A greater proportion of visitors reaching the registration action
- Fewer recurring questions about schedules, levels, prices, or locations
- Faster and safer updates by the person responsible for studio information
- Clearer promotion of undersubscribed classes or important events
- Better understanding of which channels generate inquiries
- A website the studio is willing and able to keep current
- A delivery approach Move Media can reuse profitably for another studio

The studio must help select the important outcomes. Baselines and measurement methods are needed before these become promises.

## Possible forms of the solution

### Option A — Essential information site

A small, fast website focused on classes, schedule, prices, teachers, location, frequently asked questions, and a clear contact or registration action.

Advantages:

- Fast and relatively inexpensive to deliver
- Addresses the most common visitor questions
- Good candidate for a repeatable studio offer

Limitations:

- Does not solve complex administration or student management
- Requires a simple process for keeping changing information current

### Option B — Conversion-focused studio website

An acquisition-oriented site with audience-specific class paths, trial-class calls to action, testimonials or social proof, campaign landing pages, and outcome analytics.

Advantages:

- Better connection between communication and business results
- Can test messaging and registration paths
- Stronger potential for a measurable case study

Limitations:

- Requires reliable analytics, baseline data, and studio follow-through
- Content, photos, offers, and registration workflow may increase project scope

### Option C — Website plus content workflow

A site accompanied by templates and a repeatable process for updating schedules, publishing events, and reusing information on social platforms.

Advantages:

- Addresses ongoing communication work rather than only the public pages
- Fits Move Media's workflow and automation strengths
- May support recurring service revenue

Limitations:

- Requires close study of the studio's current workflow
- More training, integration, and support may be needed

### Option D — Improve the existing presence without rebuilding

Keep the current technical system and fix only the most important content, navigation, discoverability, performance, or measurement problems.

Advantages:

- Potentially the lowest-cost and fastest intervention
- Avoids unnecessary replacement and migration

Limitations:

- Existing technology or provider constraints may limit the result
- May produce fewer reusable technical assets

## Possible first version

If discovery confirms the need for a new website, a small first version might include:

- A clear statement of what Step Up offers and for whom
- A direct path to choosing a class
- An understandable weekly schedule
- Class descriptions with level, teacher, location, and prerequisites
- Prices and registration or trial-class instructions
- Teacher or studio presentation that communicates trust and atmosphere
- Location, access, and practical information
- Frequently asked questions based on real inquiries
- One primary call to action per important visitor journey
- Basic privacy-conscious analytics and conversion measurement

Features such as student accounts, payments, attendance, complex booking, or a custom administration system should not be assumed. Existing specialist tools may already handle them better.

## Content and data to explore

Potential reusable entities include:

- Dance or movement discipline
- Class and level
- Recurring schedule and seasonal dates
- Teacher
- Location or room
- Price or membership option
- Trial or registration action
- Event or announcement
- Frequently asked question

The actual content model should follow Step Up's activities and vocabulary. It should also identify which information changes frequently, who owns it, and where its authoritative version currently lives.

## Delivery and maintenance questions

- Does Step Up already have a website, domain, analytics, or registration system?
- Who owns the existing accounts, content, images, and technical access?
- Who currently answers inquiries and updates information?
- Which information changes during the season, and how often?
- Can the website use the studio's existing scheduling or payment tools instead of replacing them?
- Does the studio need self-service editing or would a managed update service be simpler?
- What content and media already exist, and may Move Media use them?
- What is an affordable price for the studio and a sustainable delivery cost for Move Media?
- What support is expected after launch?

## Possible value for Move Media

Step Up could be a strong early project if it provides both real studio value and transferable learning. Potential returns include:

- A credible studio website case study
- A testimonial or referrals if the work succeeds
- A reusable studio discovery and content process
- Reusable class, schedule, teacher, pricing, and location components
- Evidence for fixed-scope studio website packages
- Insight into recurring services such as content updates, analytics reviews, or campaign pages

Free or discounted work should be offered only in exchange for explicit learning access, measurement cooperation, permission to publish an honest case study, or another clearly defined benefit. Hosting, maintenance, revisions, and ownership still need written boundaries.

## Assumptions

- Step Up has an important communication or acquisition problem that a website can influence
- The studio can identify a measurable business outcome rather than only requesting a visual redesign
- A decision-maker is willing to participate in discovery and provide content or access
- A useful first version can be delivered with narrow scope
- The solution can reuse existing tools instead of rebuilding studio operations
- The project can generate knowledge or components applicable to other studios

## Evidence

### Supporting

- Studio websites are part of Move Media's initial validation strategy
- Studios coordinate classes, teachers, schedules, registration, and changing information, making them relevant to Move Media's structural approach
- A real studio project can test both customer value and delivery profitability

These points support investigating the project, but they do not demonstrate a Step Up-specific need.

### Contradicting

- No audit, interview, baseline, or confirmed request from Step Up has been recorded
- The studio's existing channels may already meet its needs
- Registration or attendance may be constrained by factors a website cannot solve
- A custom website may create maintenance work without producing enough business value
- A free portfolio project could consume significant time if scope and responsibilities are unclear

## Open questions

- What is Step Up, where does it operate, and which activities does it offer?
- What business result matters most to its decision-maker this season?
- How do new students currently discover the studio and register?
- Where do prospective or current students become confused or abandon the process?
- Which questions consume the most staff time?
- Which classes, periods, or audiences need more demand?
- What does the current website or digital presence do well?
- What should not change?
- Which result can be measured before and after an intervention?
- Would Step Up participate in a documented pilot and case study?
- What is the smallest intervention that could test the main hypothesis?

## Next experiment

Conduct a discovery and journey audit before proposing a website:

1. Inventory Step Up's current public presence and the systems used for schedules, contact, and registration.
2. Attempt the main visitor journey using only public information: understand the offer, choose an appropriate class, find the price, and take the next registration step.
3. Record observed friction as one or more stories, separating facts from assumptions.
4. Interview the decision-maker about business priorities, recurring inquiries, current workflows, constraints, and willingness to measure results.
5. Select one valuable visitor or staff outcome that a small intervention could plausibly improve.
6. Compare improving the existing presence with building a new essential site.

The next decision is whether Step Up has a validated problem, a willing stakeholder, and a measurable outcome suitable for a first initiative.
