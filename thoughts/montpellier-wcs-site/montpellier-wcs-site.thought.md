# Montpellier WCS community website

## Starting point

Move Media needs a small project that is genuinely useful to a movement community while creating relationships, market knowledge, and visible evidence of its capabilities.

A local website gathering West Coast Swing information for Montpellier could be such a project. The initial idea is a neutral community entry point rather than the website of a single teacher, studio, or organizer.

No claim is currently made that the community needs this website. The need, intended users, contributors, and sustainable operating model must be validated.

## Problem or opportunity

Local dance information may be distributed across studio websites, social networks, messaging groups, event pages, and personal accounts. If this is true in Montpellier, it may create several problems:

- New dancers do not know where to begin or which information is current
- Existing dancers must monitor several channels to find classes and events
- Visitors have difficulty discovering where they can dance during a short stay
- Teachers and organizers repeatedly publish similar information in different places
- Smaller actors or one-time events are difficult to discover
- Useful community knowledge disappears in private or fast-moving conversations

The opportunity is to create a dependable public entry point that makes the local West Coast Swing ecosystem easier to understand and join without replacing the communication channels that community members already use.

## Actors and users to understand

- People considering their first West Coast Swing class
- Beginners choosing where and when to learn
- Regular local dancers looking for classes, practices, and events
- Visitors looking for a social dance or workshop in Montpellier
- Regular teachers and event teachers
- Studios offering West Coast Swing activities
- Organizers of recurring and one-time events
- Volunteers or community members who already share information

The user and the buyer are not necessarily the same person. The project also needs to identify who benefits enough to contribute time, information, promotion, or money.

## Hypothesis

A simple, current, and neutral Montpellier West Coast Swing guide can reduce the effort required to discover the community and its activities. If local professionals see it as fair and useful, they may contribute information and share it with their audiences.

Building the first version may also help Move Media:

- Learn how local dance information is produced and maintained
- Build relationships with teachers, studios, and organizers
- Demonstrate information architecture, web development, and media skills
- Produce a public case study with measurable usage
- Discover repeated needs that could become paid services

## Desired outcomes

Possible outcomes to validate include:

- Newcomers can find an appropriate first class without asking in a private group
- Dancers can see what is happening locally from one reliable page
- Teachers and organizers receive qualified visits or registrations
- Community actors spend less time answering basic recurring questions
- The community adopts the website as a shared reference
- Move Media gains useful relationships, evidence, and reusable website components

These outcomes require baselines and measurement methods before they can become initiative completion criteria.

## Possible forms of the solution

### Option A — Evergreen local guide

A small editorial website explaining where to learn and dance, who the local actors are, and how a newcomer can join the community.

Advantages:

- Small initial scope
- Relatively low maintenance
- Strong focus on newcomers and search visibility

Limitations:

- Does not fully solve recurring event discovery
- Can become outdated without clear ownership

### Option B — Guide and shared calendar

An evergreen guide combined with a regularly updated class and event calendar.

Advantages:

- More useful to active dancers
- Gives people a reason to return
- Can generate measurable referrals to organizers

Limitations:

- Requires a dependable information collection and verification workflow
- Incorrect dates or cancellations could quickly damage trust

### Option C — Community directory and contribution system

A broader hub with profiles for teachers, studios, organizers, events, and possibly service providers, supported by a submission workflow.

Advantages:

- Represents more of the ecosystem
- Creates structured data that can support future services
- Distributes contribution beyond one maintainer

Limitations:

- Greater product and governance complexity
- Requires decisions about inclusion, moderation, ranking, and commercial fairness
- Premature until the simpler information need is proven

### Option D — Curated newsletter or social account first

Test the value of aggregation through an existing communication channel before building a full website.

Advantages:

- Fast way to test interest and the editorial workload
- Meets the community in a familiar channel

Limitations:

- Information remains difficult to search and browse
- Platform dependence
- Less effective as a lasting public reference or technical portfolio project

## Possible first version

If the need is validated, a deliberately small first version might contain:

- A clear introduction to West Coast Swing in Montpellier
- A “start dancing” path for newcomers
- A current weekly class and social-dance overview
- A small directory of teachers, studios, and organizers
- Upcoming notable events
- Location and transport information
- Links to the original source or registration page for every activity
- A simple way to submit a correction or missing activity
- A visible “last verified” date where freshness matters

The first version should probably not include user accounts, proprietary ticketing, social networking, a complex content management system, or automated aggregation from every source.

## Information model to explore

Potential reusable entities include:

- Actor: teacher, studio, organizer, or community group
- Place: studio, event venue, or regular social location
- Activity: class, practice, social, workshop, performance, or competition
- Schedule: recurring or one-time date and time
- Level or audience
- Registration or source link
- Verification status and last-checked date

The model should follow real community language and workflows rather than imposing these categories without research.

## Governance and sustainability questions

- Who decides which actors and activities are included?
- What does “neutral” mean when actors compete for students?
- Can every listing link back to an authoritative source?
- Who verifies changes, cancellations, and seasonal schedules?
- Can contributors update their own information without creating moderation work?
- What happens when information is disputed?
- Who owns the domain, content, data, and ongoing responsibility?
- What level of maintenance can Move Media sustain while the project is free?

## Possible business value for Move Media

The community website should not rely on speculative advertising revenue. More plausible early value may come from:

- Relationships and referrals generated through the project
- A credible community and directory website case study
- Reusable components for other cities or movement communities
- Paid profile, website, content, or event services requested by listed professionals
- Sponsorship or maintenance support, provided neutrality remains credible
- Evidence for a narrowly scoped directory or event communication product

Any commercial model must make sponsorship and paid placement transparent and must not undermine trust in the completeness or ordering of community information.

## Assumptions

- Important Montpellier WCS information is currently fragmented or difficult to verify
- Newcomers and active dancers experience enough friction to value a shared reference
- Local professionals would accept or support a neutral community website
- Enough public information exists to launch a useful first version
- The maintenance workload can be constrained or distributed
- A community project can create meaningful visibility and relationships for Move Media

## Evidence

### Supporting

- The broader Move Media project has identified local community websites as a plausible entry project
- Movement communities commonly coordinate several types of classes and events, creating an information-management problem worth investigating
- An initial public-web audit found current WCS activity distributed across independent school sites, municipal directories, HelloAsso, Meetup, event aggregators, and social media. See [Montpellier-area WCS actors — online audit](wcs-actors-online-audit.md).
- Current public sources identify recurring or recent activity in Montpellier, Mauguio, Milhaud, Saint-Thibéry, Saint-Aunès, Saint-Gély-du-Fesc, Saint-Jean-de-Védas, and Fabrègues. No actor was confirmed yet in Baillargues or Vendargues.
- A volunteer “WCS Montpellier” aggregation website previously attempted to collect courses, socials, stages, and events, but its visible archive ends in 2017. This is evidence both of prior perceived value and of the sustainability risk.

These points motivate research but do not yet validate the Montpellier-specific need.

### Contradicting

- Only an initial public-source discovery audit has been recorded; no user or actor interviews have been conducted yet
- Existing community channels may already solve the problem well enough
- Community members may prefer private groups, personal recommendations, or established social platforms
- Maintaining accurate information may cost more time than the value the project creates

## Open questions

- What are the current public and private sources of Montpellier WCS information?
- Which source do newcomers find first, and what can they not understand from it?
- What questions are repeatedly asked in local groups or direct messages?
- Which information changes most often?
- Do teachers and organizers want a shared source, and what would make them trust it?
- Is there an existing community owner, site, calendar, or project that should be supported instead of replaced?
- Which audience should the first version serve: newcomers, active dancers, visitors, or professionals?
- Who would maintain the site after launch?
- Which measurable behavior would demonstrate that the site is useful?

## Next experiment

Run a small discovery audit before designing the website:

1. List the public sources currently used to find Montpellier WCS classes and events.
2. Attempt three realistic journeys using only those sources: find a first class, find somewhere to dance this week, and plan a visit from outside Montpellier.
3. Record missing, conflicting, outdated, or duplicated information as stories.
4. Speak with at least one newcomer, one regular dancer, and two local professionals about how they find and publish information.
5. Decide which single audience and information problem has the strongest evidence.

This experiment is now Milestone 1 of the resulting initiative. Its findings will confirm or revise the launch scope, baselines, provider-coverage target, and stop conditions before public release.
